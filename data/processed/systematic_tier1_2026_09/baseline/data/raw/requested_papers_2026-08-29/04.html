Exploiting the Source-Asymmetry Confidence Gap for Generalizable AI-Generated Image Detection

Ziyang Zheng, Weiyan Chen, Yao Xiao, Zijie Cao, Dongyu Zhang, Pengxu Wei; Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR) Findings, 2026, pp. 8715-8724

Abstract

Existing AI-generated image (AIGI) detection methods are trained on images synthesized by only a few generators, resulting in poor generalization to the ever-evolving domain of unseen generative models. In response to this limitation, recent work leverages large Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) to obtain more robust representations, yet typically relies on computationally expensive strategies, e.g., introducing additional large-scale synthetic datasets or employing complex training pipelines. In this paper, we devise a lightweight VFM-based strategy for generalized AIGI detection. Our key insight is a source asymmetry phenomenon: during training, models are exposed to diverse real data, while the fake images are synthesized by only a few generators. We verify that this class-diversity asymmetry causes a fine-tuned VFM to learn a more generalizable representation for the real class. This mechanism yields our key finding: the Source-Asymmetry Confidence Gap (SACG), a consistent and exploitable separation in predictive confidence where unseen real images yield systematically higher confidence than unseen fakes. Leveraging this insight, we introduce a lightweight, post-hoc strategy that employs a specialized confidence metric (GEN score) to amplify this gap and applies a conditional decision rule calibrated only on the real images for stable detection. On the Community Forensics benchmark (21 distinct generative models), our method achieves state-of-the-art results, surpassing the next-best baseline by +6.82% in accuracy.
