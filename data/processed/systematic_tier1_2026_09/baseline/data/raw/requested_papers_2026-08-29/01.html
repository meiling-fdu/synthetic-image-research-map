A Hybrid CLIP-Diffusion Architecture for Transparent Deepfake Detection and Explainability Evaluation

Andreas Specker, Manjunatha Veerappa, Thomas Golda, Nadia Burkart, Dominik Alveen, Anna Wilhelm; Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR) Workshops, 2026, pp. 467-476

Abstract

Generative diffusion models can synthesize highly realistic images that are increasingly difficult to distinguish from real content, raising substantial risks through misuse as deepfakes. This paper studies how to build deepfake detectors that both generalize to unseen image generators and provide transparent explanations of their decisions. We first extend the SynRIS benchmark to SynRIS++, adding fakes from five recent diffusion models and carefully matched real counterparts via reverse image search, yielding over 120k images from 18 generators. Guided by recent findings, we propose a hybrid CLIP-Diffusion architecture that combines (i) a frozen CLIP backbone with a classifier operating on intermediate features and (ii) a diffusion-based reconstruction error head, fused by a learnable ensemble. To obtain a conservative estimate of cross-generator generalization, we train exclusively on real images and deepfakes from Stable Diffusion v1, while evaluating on SynRIS++, which spans a broad range of modern generators. Our method achieves state-of-the-art performance on this challenging benchmark, substantially improving AP and AUROC over prior work. Finally, we systematically evaluate five post-hoc XAI methods for deepfake detection along multiple dimensions: explanatory accuracy, stability, robustness to noise and resolution, and scalability. The findings reveal practical trade-offs between interpretability and computational cost. Project Page: https://github.com/speckean/Transparent-Deepfake-Detection
