## 1. Adopt the following authoritative scope rules

### Policy A — Watermark / active fingerprint / proactive provenance

EXCLUDE.

Papers whose primary forensic mechanism depends on intentionally embedding, injecting, or controlling a watermark, fingerprint, signature, provenance token, credential, or similar active signal are outside the scope of this Research Map.

This includes:

* watermark embedding
* watermark detection/verification
* watermark robustness
* watermark removal/evasion
* active fingerprint injection
* generator-side fingerprint construction
* proactive provenance
* cryptographic provenance
* ownership/copyright protection
* content authentication mechanisms
* C2PA-like provenance mechanisms
* recovery of deliberately embedded signatures

Do NOT include such papers merely because:

* they report a detection accuracy;
* they recover an embedded signal;
* they identify a source using an intentionally inserted fingerprint;
* they evaluate robustness against attacks.

The Research Map focuses on forensic inference from synthetic-image content/traces, not intentionally embedded provenance mechanisms.

### Narrow exception

If a paper contains a genuinely independent passive synthetic-image forensic method that:

* does not require the watermark/fingerprint/provenance mechanism;
* is independently evaluated;
* and itself fits Detection, Source Attribution, or Localization;

then that independent passive contribution may be reviewed separately.

The presence of a watermark component must not by itself qualify the paper.

## 2. Pure deepfake / face-forensics policy

EXCLUDE pure deepfake work.

Do NOT include papers solely because they perform:

* face-swap detection
* face reenactment detection
* facial manipulation detection
* facial forgery localization
* identity manipulation detection
* video deepfake detection
* frame-level deepfake detection

A method being applicable to individual video frames is NOT sufficient.

Deepfake is not, by itself, part of the target scope.

### Include only when there is a substantive synthetic-image component

A paper may remain potentially in scope only when it substantively studies:

* fully AI-generated images;
* diffusion/GAN-generated imagery;
* generative editing;
* partial synthetic regions;
* synthetic-image source attribution;
* general AI-generated image detection/localization;

and the deepfake experiments are only one component of that broader synthetic-image forensic contribution.

Do not classify a pure face-forensics paper as in scope simply because a generative model created the face manipulation.

## 3. Generative editing / partial synthesis

INCLUDE when substantive.

The following remain within scope:

* diffusion-based image editing
* generative inpainting
* text-guided image editing
* partial AI-generated regions
* mixed real/synthetic images
* generative-edit localization
* detection of AI-generated modifications

provided that the contribution directly fits:

* Detection
* Source Attribution
* Localization

This is distinct from classical manipulation.

## 4. Classical manipulation

EXCLUDE classical-only image forensics.

Exclude new papers focused only on:

* copy-move
* splicing
* classical compositing
* retouching
* conventional Photoshop manipulation
* traditional image forgery

A mixed benchmark or method may qualify only if AI-generated / generative manipulation is a substantive, independently evaluated component.

Do not expand the map into general image-forensics literature.

## 5. Passive source attribution remains core scope

INCLUDE genuine passive image-to-generator/source inference, such as:

* source generator attribution
* model attribution
* generator identification
* reference-based attribution
* passive fingerprint analysis from naturally occurring generation traces

The key distinction is:

PASSIVE forensic trace
vs.
INTENTIONALLY EMBEDDED fingerprint.

Passive naturally occurring model traces remain in scope.

Active injected signatures do not.

## 6. Passive AI-generated-image detector robustness/evasion

Papers analyzing or attacking passive AI-generated-image detectors may remain potentially in scope when their central object of study is an in-scope synthetic-image detector.

For example:

* robustness of AI-generated image detection
* distortion sensitivity
* adversarial evasion of passive synthetic-image detectors
* detector bias/generalization analysis

Do not extend this rule to watermark attacks or generic deepfake detector attacks that are otherwise outside project scope.

## 7. Multimodal work

Include only when there is a substantive independently evaluated IMAGE forensic contribution concerning AI-generated/synthetic images.

Exclude papers where:

* image is merely one input modality;
* the primary task is misinformation classification;
* the image branch has no standalone synthetic-image forensic contribution.

## 8. Specialized domains

Domain specialization alone is not exclusionary.

Synthetic-image forensic work involving:

* biomedical imagery
* scientific images
* Western blots
* remote sensing
* other specialized image domains

may remain in scope when the actual task is AI-generated/synthetic image Detection, Attribution, or Localization.

The criterion is the forensic task, not the application domain.
